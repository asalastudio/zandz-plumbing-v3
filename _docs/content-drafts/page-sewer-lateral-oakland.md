## 6 · Body copy

### Opening paragraph (~120 words)

If you're searching for sewer lateral repair in Oakland, you're usually doing it for one of three reasons: a real-estate inspector flagged your lateral during a sale, you're getting recurring backups that augering won't fix, or the city sent you a Private Sewer Lateral (PSL) compliance notice. In every case, you need someone who can do the WHOLE job, not just the part on your property. Z and Z Plumbing is one of the few East Bay contractors that holds both the C-36 plumbing license AND the A General Engineering license (CSLB #896116), which is what gives us legal authority to work in the public right-of-way between your property line and the city main. That's the part of your lateral other plumbers must subcontract. Call us at [(510) 708-4237](tel:5107084237) for same-day inspection.

### H2 · What Oakland's sewer lateral compliance ordinance requires (~220 words)

Oakland's Private Sewer Lateral (PSL) ordinance is enforced by the East Bay Municipal Utility District (EBMUD) under the Wastewater Capacity Charge Program. A compliance certificate is required at three trigger points:

1. **Point of sale**. Most common. Before you can close on a sale of property in Oakland, the lateral has to be inspected and certified. The certificate is valid for 20 years from issue.
2. **Major remodel**. If your remodel adds bathrooms or significantly increases plumbing fixtures, EBMUD may require compliance verification as part of the permit.
3. **Failed lateral**. If a backup or city sewer inspection identifies your lateral as the source, you have a fixed window (typically 21–90 days) to bring it into compliance.

The compliance process has three steps: camera inspection from cleanout to city main, repair of any failed sections (joint failures, root intrusion, partial collapses, settling, offset connections), and final certificate filing with EBMUD's Wastewater Capacity Charge office.

**The catch most homeowners discover too late:** the lateral runs from your house, across your yard, and continues UNDER THE SIDEWALK AND INTO THE STREET to where it joins Oakland's city main. Failures often happen in that street-side section because of tree roots, ground settling near the curb, and the older clay pipe Oakland used for decades. Repairing the street-side portion legally requires the A General Engineering classification. Without it, the contractor has to subcontract the right-of-way work to a separate company, which doubles coordination and adds a markup.

### H2 · Why two licenses on one crew matters (~180 words)

The CSLB issues a separate classification for each type of work a contractor is qualified to do. The two relevant ones here:

- **C-36 (Plumbing)**. Covers plumbing inside your property line. Sinks, fixtures, water heaters, drain lines on your side of the curb. Every California plumber has this.
- **A (General Engineering)**. Covers civil engineering work in the public right-of-way. Trenching in the street. Excavating to the city main. Work on the public-utility-owned portion of your service connection. **Most plumbers do NOT hold this license.** Holding it requires a separate exam, four years of qualifying experience in civil engineering work, and Oakland Public Works approval.

Z and Z Plumbing has held both since 2012. CSLB License #896116. You can verify directly at [cslb.ca.gov](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/onlineservices/checklicenseII/checklicense.aspx).

What this means for your sewer lateral job: one crew, one truck, one permit pull at Oakland Public Works, one set of inspections, one quote, one invoice. Not a primary plumber-plus-subcontractor chain that adds a week to your timeline and 10–15% to your bill.

### H2 · The five most common Oakland sewer lateral failures we repair (~240 words)

After 23 years of pulling laterals in Oakland (the BuildZoom record shows 447 permits on file with Oakland Public Works alone, most of them lateral work), the failure modes are predictable:

**1. Offset joints in the parkway strip.** The parkway is the planting strip between sidewalk and curb. Heavy trees there cause ground settling that pulls clay joints apart. Most common in Rockridge, Temescal, Montclair, and the older parts of Glenview. Repair: spot dig + new joint, or trenchless reline if the run is otherwise sound.

**2. Root intrusion at clay-to-iron transitions.** Oakland homes built in the 1920s–1950s often have clay laterals connecting to a cast-iron stack near the foundation. The transition joint is a magnet for root intrusion. Hydrojetting clears it temporarily; permanent fix is a relined or replaced transition.

**3. Partial collapse near the city main.** The portion under the street that connects to Oakland's main is where decades of traffic load and settling do the most damage. Common in the older flatland streets. Requires excavation in the right-of-way. The A General Engineering work.

**4. Saddle connections that have failed.** Older Oakland properties were tapped into the main with a clay or iron saddle. When the saddle cracks, you get sewer backup AND street infiltration. Repair requires Oakland Public Works coordination, an A-license excavation, and a proper main-line tap installed.

**5. Wyes installed during prior renovations that don't meet current code.** When a previous owner added a bathroom or kitchen, the new line was sometimes tied in with a code-deficient wye fitting. The connection holds for years, then fails. We re-cut the connection and install code-compliant fittings during the repair.

### H2 · What the process looks like, end to end (~200 words)

**Day 1 (typically same-day or next-day):** Camera inspection. We run a CCTV camera from the cleanout out to the city main, document every failure point with location and severity, and send you a recorded video. Cost: $400–$800 depending on whether excavation is needed to access a cleanout. This cost rolls into the repair quote if you proceed.

**Day 1–3:** Written quote with a not-to-exceed price, clearly broken into property-side work (C-36) and right-of-way work (A General Engineering). If trenchless reline is an option, we quote that alternative too with the cost-and-tradeoff explanation.

**Day 3–5:** Permits pulled with Oakland Public Works. We handle this directly because we're a permitted A contractor. You don't coordinate.

**Day 5–10:** The repair. Most lateral jobs are 2–4 working days on-site. Trenchless relines can finish in 1 day if the lateral is otherwise sound. Open-trench repair in the street adds 1–2 days for backfill and pavement restoration to Oakland standards.

**Day 10–14:** EBMUD compliance certificate filed. You receive the certificate by mail, valid for 20 years. Your real-estate transaction (if applicable) can close.

## 8 · FAQs (with FAQPage schema)

**Q: Do I need an EBMUD sewer lateral compliance certificate to sell my Oakland home?**
A: In nearly all cases, yes. Oakland's Private Sewer Lateral ordinance requires a compliance certificate at point-of-sale unless the home was built after 2007 and has documented certification from construction. The certificate is valid for 20 years. We handle inspection, any required repair, and certificate filing.

**Q: How long does the sewer lateral inspection take?**
A: The inspection itself is 60–90 minutes. We arrive with a CCTV camera rig, locate or excavate to the cleanout, and run the camera the full length of the lateral to the city main. You get a recorded video and a written summary the same day.

**Q: What happens if my lateral fails the inspection?**
A: You receive a written list of specific failure points with location and recommended repair. You typically have 21–90 days from the failure date to complete repairs and re-file for certification, depending on whether the trigger was a point-of-sale, a remodel permit, or a city-issued notice.

**Q: Why do you keep mentioning the A General Engineering license? What does it actually do for me?**
A: Sewer laterals run from your house, through your yard, and continue under the sidewalk and into the street to connect to Oakland's main sewer line. The portion in the public right-of-way (sidewalk and street) legally requires the A General Engineering license to repair. Most plumbing companies in Oakland only hold the C-36 plumbing license, so they have to sub-contract the right-of-way portion to another contractor. We hold both licenses, so one crew handles the entire repair start to finish.

**Q: What's the difference between trenchless and open-trench sewer lateral repair?**
A: Trenchless inserts a new pipe inside the existing one (cured-in-place pipe or pipe bursting). Minimal digging, faster, less yard damage. Works only if the existing lateral has the right alignment and structural integrity. Open-trench digs up the existing lateral and replaces it directly. Slower and more invasive but works regardless of existing pipe condition. We quote both options when both are viable so you can pick.

**Q: Do you handle the Oakland Public Works permitting?**
A: Yes, as a CSLB-licensed A General Engineering contractor we pull the encroachment permit and coordinate inspections with Oakland Public Works directly. You don't talk to the city. We do.

**Q: How long does the full repair take?**
A: From first phone call to filed EBMUD certificate: typically 7–14 business days, sometimes less if the lateral is in good shape and we just need to certify. The actual excavation and repair work is usually 2–4 working days on-site.

**Q: Are you EBMUD-approved?**
A: EBMUD doesn't certify contractors. It certifies completed laterals. What matters for the contractor is the CSLB licensing (C-36 and A General Engineering) and Oakland Public Works permitting authority, both of which we hold.
