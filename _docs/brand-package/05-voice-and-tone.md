# 05 · Voice and Tone

**Status:** Locked 2026-05-11

## Voice principles

Z and Z's voice is the way a senior contractor talks to a homeowner who just discovered a serious plumbing problem. Direct, calm, expert, no upsell theater.

### 1. Direct

Plumbing problems are urgent. Customers do not have time for marketing copy. Say what the problem is, what we will do, what it costs, when we can be there. Sentences are short. Paragraphs are short.

Bad: "Our team of dedicated professionals takes pride in delivering an unparalleled level of service to homeowners across the East Bay community."

Good: "Call us. We answer 24/7. A licensed plumber is at your door in two hours, usually less."

### 2. Confident

No hedging. No "if possible." No "depending on your situation." Customers want certainty. Where we can give a real range, we give the range. Where we cannot, we say "we will tell you on site after we look at it." We do not promise things we cannot deliver, but we do not pre-apologize either.

Bad: "We might be able to fix it, depending on a few things. We will try our best to schedule a visit as soon as possible."

Good: "We can fix this. We need 20 minutes on site to give you a quote, and we have a truck in your neighborhood today."

### 3. Expert without jargon

We know more about Oakland sewer laterals than almost anyone. We talk like the expert in the room. But we explain technical terms when they matter. "Sewer lateral" gets explained on its dedicated page. "EBMUD compliance certificate" gets explained too. We do not assume the customer knows the trade.

Bad: "Your PSL needs a CCTV camera inspection before EBMUD will issue compliance."

Good: "Before you can sell your Oakland home, the city needs proof your sewer lateral is in good shape. We run a camera through it, document any problems, fix what needs fixing, and file the compliance certificate. Typical timeline: 7 to 14 days."

### 4. Local

We are an East Bay business. We mention neighborhoods. We mention real-world local context. We do not pretend to be a national chain.

Bad: "Plumbing services across the greater San Francisco Bay Area."

Good: "We work in Oakland, San Leandro, Berkeley, Alameda, Hayward, Castro Valley, Richmond, Lafayette, Pinole, and Emeryville. We are based at our shop on Teagarden Street in San Leandro."

### 5. Provable

Every claim has proof. License numbers. Years in business. Number of permits on record. Real customer quotes. We never use "best," "top-rated," "premier," or other adjectives that cannot be defended.

Bad: "The premier plumbing contractor in the East Bay."

Good: "CSLB License #896116 since 2003. 447 permits on record with Oakland Public Works. 4.6 stars across 19 Google reviews and 4.5 stars across 238 Yelp reviews."

## Tone by surface

### Homepage hero

Confident, direct, single-purpose. The customer is here to find a plumber. Get them to the action.

### Service pages

Expert. Explain the service. Explain when a customer needs it. Explain what it costs. Explain why two licenses matters when it matters (sewer lateral, gas line, water line) and why it does not matter when it does not (faucet repair).

### Service area pages

Local. Mention neighborhoods. Mention the housing era and the common plumbing issues for that city. Avoid generic city-template content.

### Blog

Educational. Plumbing 101 for homeowners. Real questions real customers ask. Each post answers one question completely. Never thin "5 tips" listicles.

### GBP description

Trust signals first. License. Years. Service area. Differentiator. Phone.

### Booking widget

Conversational. Short prompts. "What's going on?" not "Please describe the nature of your plumbing concern."

### Email confirmations

Warm but brief. Reassure the customer they will hear back. Give the callback expectation in time terms ("within 30 minutes during business hours, by 9 AM tomorrow if after hours").

### SMS review requests

Personal-feeling. From Jay or Seif personally. "Hi [name], it's Jay at Z and Z Plumbing. Hope you are doing well. If we did good work today, a Google review really helps us out. Here is a quick link: [link]. Thanks. -Jay"

## Banned phrases

Permanent ban. Do not use these in any customer-facing surface.

- "Look no further"
- "In today's fast-paced world"
- "We pride ourselves on"
- "State-of-the-art"
- "Top-notch"
- "Unparalleled"
- "Premier" (as an adjective applied to ourselves)
- "Best" (as a self-applied superlative)
- "Family-owned and operated" (overused; if true, just say "Z and Z is the Zareef family business")
- "Your trusted local plumber" (the customer decides if we are trusted, not us)
- "Five-star service" (let the reviews speak)
- Em-dashes (use periods, commas, colons, semicolons, or "and")

## Approved phrases (the muscle memory of the brand)

Use these recurringly:

- "The pros other plumbers call"
- "Two licenses. One crew."
- "We answer the phone"
- "Same-day service"
- "C-36 plumbing plus A General Engineering"
- "We legally handle the entire job, including the part in the street"
- "We back our work"
- "We will quote you up front, before any work starts"
- "We are based in San Leandro and serve the East Bay"

## When the voice slips

If a draft starts to read as flowery, generic, or chain-restaurant, stop and rewrite. The fix is almost always: cut the adjectives, shorten the sentences, add a real number, name a real neighborhood.
